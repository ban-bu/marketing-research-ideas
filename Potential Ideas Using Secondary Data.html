二、具体选题与研究设计

1. 时尚趋势

Predicting Fashion or Shaping It? How Trend Forecasts Influence Brand Adoption
目标期刊：Marketing Science
背景
WGSN 等趋势预测机构被大量时尚品牌、设计师和零售企业共同使用，因此其预测可能不仅反映未来，也可能影响企业下一季的设计决策。当许多品牌依据相同预测行动时，原本分散的设计选择可能逐渐同步，预测本身也可能推动某些趋势真正形成。现有研究更多关注趋势扩散和预测准确性，较少讨论趋势预测机构是否会通过协调品牌预期而改变市场本身。
核心问题
时尚趋势预测是在提前发现趋势，还是会推动品牌共同采用某些设计元素，从而塑造趋势本身？
变量与数据
变量角色变量定义数据来源
因变量 1Trend Adoption
品牌下一季是否采用被预测的趋势元素		Vogue Archive / WWD + 其他合法时尚资料
因变量 2Cross-brand Similarity
预测后不同品牌在设计上的相似程度		Vogue Archive / WWD + 视觉特征计算
核心自变量Trend Forecast Strength / Forecast Surprise
WGSN 对某一趋势的预测强度，或预测强度相对于当期流行程度的超前程度		WGSN
当前趋势水平预测发布时，该趋势已经在市场中出现的程度Vogue Archive / WWD + 其他时尚资料
核心关系趋势预测越强 → 下一季采用概率越高
多个品牌共同响应 → 品牌之间的设计相似度可能上升		由上述数据合并构建
模型

先检验某一趋势被强烈预测后，下一季采用它的品牌是否更多。再进一步观察这些共同预测是否伴随品牌之间更高的设计相似度，从而判断预测机构是否可能在“发现趋势”之外发挥市场协调作用。

\[ \Pr(\text{Adopt}=1) = \operatorname{logit}^{-1} \left( \beta_1 \text{Trend Forecast Strength} + \text{Controls} \right) \]
贡献
  • 把时尚趋势研究从“预测是否准确”推进到“预测是否会塑造市场”。
  • 将趋势预测机构理解为可能影响品牌决策的市场协调者，而不仅是信息提供者。
  • 解释为什么大量品牌依赖相同趋势信息时，可能出现更强的同步跟随和产品趋同。
目标期刊
Marketing Science

2. 全球品牌的语言负债

Lost in Translation? The Linguistic Liability of Global Brand Names
目标期刊：Journal of Marketing Research (JMR)
背景
全球品牌通常沿用统一名称，以维持识别度、传播效率和全球一致性。但同一个名称在不同语言中的发音难度、音节结构、拼写熟悉度和语音联想并不相同。已有研究表明，这些语言特征会影响消费者判断，但证据主要来自实验。尚不清楚的是，这种语言摩擦是否足以影响真实市场表现，以及企业通过音译、意译或其他本地化命名能否缓解这一影响。
核心问题
同一个全球品牌名在不同语言市场中的语言摩擦，是否会降低真实市场表现？本地化命名能否减弱这一影响？
变量与数据
变量角色变量定义数据来源
因变量品牌市场表现
品牌市场份额、市场份额增长、品类排名		Euromonitor Passport
核心自变量Linguistic Friction
品牌名称在当地语言中的发音难度、音系适配度、音节复杂度等		开放语言数据库 + 自行计算
调节变量Name Localization
名称不变、音译、意译、音义结合或完全本地化		品牌官网、当地市场公开资料 + 人工编码
核心关系语言摩擦越高 → 品牌市场表现越弱
本地化命名预计会削弱这一负面关系		由上述数据合并构建
模型

比较同一个品牌在不同语言市场中的表现，同时控制品牌自身、当地市场、品类和年份差异。这样可以尽量排除品牌全球热度和当地市场环境的影响。若能找到真实的本地改名案例，还可以进一步比较改名前后的市场表现。

\[ \text{Market Share} = \beta_1 \text{Linguistic Friction} + \beta_2 \text{Name Localization} + \beta_3 \bigl(\text{Friction}\times\text{Localization}\bigr) + \text{Controls} + \varepsilon \]
贡献
  • 将品牌命名研究从“什么名字更容易被消费者喜欢”推进到“同一个名字为什么在不同市场具有不同经济价值”。
  • 揭示全球统一命名的隐藏成本，即品牌一致性和传播效率之外，还存在语言适配带来的市场差异。
  • 为企业判断何时坚持全球统一名称、何时进行本地化命名提供直接依据。
目标期刊
Journal of Marketing Research (JMR)

3. 并购后的品牌迁移

Owned Overnight, Rebranded Over Time: The Market Consequences of Post-Acquisition Brand Migration
目标期刊：Journal of Marketing (JM)
背景
并购可以在一天内改变法律所有权，但消费者心中的品牌身份通常不会同步改变。收购方需要决定是保留目标品牌、增加母品牌背书，还是逐步用收购方品牌替换，同时还要决定这一过程推进多快。现有研究更多关注财务整合和交易绩效，品牌身份迁移如何影响被收购品牌的真实市场表现仍缺乏系统的大样本证据。
核心问题
收购后，被收购品牌应该改多少、改多快，才能最大限度保留原有品牌价值？
变量与数据
变量角色变量定义数据来源
因变量并购后品牌表现
被收购品牌的市场份额和市场份额增长		Euromonitor Passport
核心自变量 1Migration Depth
品牌身份改变程度：保留 → 母品牌背书 → 完全替换		公司公告、品牌官网、Factiva + 人工编码
核心自变量 2Migration Speed
从并购完成到品牌身份发生改变所经历的时间		并购完成日：LSEG Workspace – Deals
品牌迁移日：公司公告、Factiva
调节变量Pre-acquisition Brand Equity
并购前目标品牌原有市场实力		Euromonitor Passport
控制变量交易规模、交易时间、收购方与目标方特征等LSEG Workspace – Deals
核心关系迁移程度与迁移速度 → 并购后品牌表现
原有品牌资产越强，过快或过深迁移的代价可能越大		由上述数据合并构建
模型

以品牌真正发生改名、背书或替换的时间为界，比较迁移前后的市场表现，并比较不同迁移程度和迁移速度。若同一个被收购品牌在不同国家采用不同迁移时间，还可以利用这种时间差进一步识别品牌迁移的影响。

\[ \text{Market Share} = \beta_1 \text{Migration Depth} + \beta_2 \text{Migration Speed} + \beta_3 \text{Target Brand Equity} + \text{Interactions} + \text{Controls} + \varepsilon \]
贡献
  • 区分法律所有权转移与消费者品牌身份转移，强调两者并不是同一个过程，也未必以相同速度发生。
  • 把并购后的“整合”具体化为品牌迁移程度和迁移速度两个可观察的营销决策。
  • 直接回答并购后的核心管理问题，即被收购品牌应该保留多少原有身份，以及何时进行更深度的整合。
目标期刊
Journal of Marketing (JM)

4. 品牌防火墙

The Brand Firewall: How Brand Architecture Contains Corporate Reputation Spillovers
目标期刊：Journal of Marketing (JM)
背景
品牌架构决定企业声誉在旗下品牌之间如何传递。母公司与产品品牌联系越紧密，企业的正向声誉和品牌资产越容易被共享，但一旦发生环境、劳工、治理或商业伦理争议，负面影响也可能更快扩散。现有研究分别讨论了品牌架构和危机溢出，但较少直接检验既有品牌架构是否会系统性地改变企业负面事件对真实市场需求的影响。
核心问题
品牌架构能否隔离企业负面事件对旗下品牌需求的冲击？
变量与数据
变量角色变量定义数据来源
因变量品牌市场表现
企业争议发生前后，旗下品牌市场份额及其变化		Euromonitor Passport
核心自变量Corporate Controversy
环境、劳工、治理、商业伦理等企业负面事件及发生时间		LSEG Workspace
调节变量Parent–Brand Linkage
产品品牌与母公司之间的可见关联程度		品牌名称、官网、包装、公司资料 + 人工编码
品牌归属Brand–Parent Mapping
每个消费品牌对应的母公司		LSEG Workspace + 公司资料
核心关系企业负面事件 → 旗下品牌表现下降
品牌与母公司联系越强 → 负面影响预计越大		由上述数据合并构建
模型

以企业负面事件为时间节点，比较事件发生前后旗下品牌的市场表现。更重要的是，在同一家公司内部比较与母公司联系强弱不同的品牌，以判断品牌架构是否会放大或减弱声誉传导。

\[ \text{Market Share} = \beta_1 \bigl( \text{Post-Controversy} \times \text{Parent--Brand Linkage} \bigr) + \text{Controls} + \varepsilon \]
贡献
  • 将品牌架构从“如何组织品牌组合”的静态问题推进为企业如何配置声誉风险的动态机制。
  • 揭示品牌资产共享与负面声誉隔离之间的结构性权衡，即品牌联系越紧密，收益和风险都更容易共同传递。
  • 为多品牌企业在品牌组合设计、ESG 风险和危机管理中的长期决策提供依据。
目标期刊
Journal of Marketing (JM)

2. Research Ideas and Designs

1. Fashion Trends

Predicting Fashion or Shaping It? How Trend Forecasts Influence Brand Adoption
Target Journal: Marketing Science
Background
Trend forecasters such as WGSN are widely used by fashion brands, designers, and retailers. Their forecasts may therefore do more than anticipate the future because they can also influence next-season design decisions. When many brands respond to the same forecast, otherwise decentralized choices may become more synchronized and the forecast itself may help a trend emerge. Prior work has examined trend diffusion and forecast accuracy, but less is known about whether forecasting intermediaries actively shape the market they describe.
Core Question
Do fashion forecasts merely identify emerging trends, or do they help create them by encouraging brands to adopt the same design elements?
Variables and Data
RoleVariable / DefinitionData Source
Dependent variable 1Trend Adoption
Whether a brand adopts the forecast trend in the following season		Vogue Archive / WWD + other lawful fashion sources
Dependent variable 2Cross-brand Similarity
Similarity in design across brands after the forecast		Vogue Archive / WWD + constructed visual measures
Main explanatory variableTrend Forecast Strength / Forecast Surprise
WGSN forecast emphasis, or forecast strength relative to the trend's current prevalence		WGSN
Current trend prevalenceHow common the focal trend already is when the forecast is issuedVogue Archive / WWD + other fashion sources
Core relationshipStronger forecasts → higher next-season adoption
Shared responses across brands → greater design similarity		Constructed by merging the sources above
Empirical Approach

First examine whether strongly forecast trends are adopted by more brands in the following season. Then test whether common forecasts are followed by greater similarity across brands. Together, the two analyses help distinguish simple prediction from a broader market-coordination role.

\[ \Pr(\text{Adopt}=1) = \operatorname{logit}^{-1} \left( \beta_1 \text{Trend Forecast Strength} + \text{Controls} \right) \]
Contribution
  • Moves fashion-trend research beyond forecast accuracy to ask whether forecasts actively shape the market.
  • Positions trend forecasters as potential market coordinators rather than passive information providers.
  • Explains why reliance on common trend information may synchronize brand decisions and increase product convergence.
Target Journal
Marketing Science

2. Linguistic Liability of Global Brand Names

Lost in Translation? The Linguistic Liability of Global Brand Names
Target Journal: Journal of Marketing Research (JMR)
Background
Global brands often keep the same name across countries to preserve recognition, communication efficiency, and consistency. Yet the same name can differ sharply across languages in pronunciation difficulty, syllabic structure, spelling familiarity, and phonological associations. Prior research shows that these features can shape consumer judgments, but most evidence comes from experiments. It remains unclear whether such linguistic frictions are large enough to affect real market performance and whether local naming can offset them.
Core Question
Does linguistic friction reduce a global brand's market performance across language markets, and can local naming weaken that effect?
Variables and Data
RoleVariable / DefinitionData Source
Dependent variableBrand market performance
Market share, market-share growth, and category rank		Euromonitor Passport
Main explanatory variableLinguistic Friction
Pronunciation difficulty, phonological fit, syllabic complexity, and related measures in each language market		Open linguistic databases + constructed measures
ModeratorName Localization
Unchanged name, transliteration, translation, hybrid form, or fully localized name		Brand websites, local-market materials + hand coding
Core relationshipGreater linguistic friction → weaker market performance
Local naming is expected to weaken this negative relationship		Constructed by merging the sources above
Empirical Approach

Compare the same brand across different language markets while accounting for brand-specific, local-market, category, and year differences. This helps separate linguistic effects from broader changes in global brand popularity or local market conditions. Where a real local-name change can be identified, performance can also be compared before and after the change.

\[ \text{Market Share} = \beta_1 \text{Linguistic Friction} + \beta_2 \text{Name Localization} + \beta_3 \bigl(\text{Friction}\times\text{Localization}\bigr) + \text{Controls} + \varepsilon \]
Contribution
  • Moves brand-naming research from asking which names are generally more appealing to asking why the same name carries different economic value across markets.
  • Identifies a hidden cost of global naming standardization alongside its benefits in consistency and communication efficiency.
  • Provides a clearer basis for deciding when firms should preserve one global name and when local adaptation is likely to create value.
Target Journal
Journal of Marketing Research (JMR)

3. Post-Acquisition Brand Migration

Owned Overnight, Rebranded Over Time: The Market Consequences of Post-Acquisition Brand Migration
Target Journal: Journal of Marketing (JM)
Background
An acquisition can transfer legal ownership overnight, but consumer-held brand identity rarely changes that quickly. The acquirer must decide whether to retain, endorse, or replace the target brand and how quickly to make that transition. Existing research focuses largely on financial integration and deal outcomes. Systematic large-sample evidence on how brand-identity migration affects the acquired brand's market performance remains limited.
Core Question
How far and how fast should an acquirer change an acquired brand while preserving its existing market value?
Variables and Data
RoleVariable / DefinitionData Source
Dependent variablePost-acquisition brand performance
Market share and market-share growth of the acquired brand		Euromonitor Passport
Main explanatory variable 1Migration Depth
Degree of identity change: retain → endorse → replace		Company announcements, brand websites, Factiva + hand coding
Main explanatory variable 2Migration Speed
Time between deal completion and a visible change in brand identity		Deal completion date: LSEG Workspace – Deals
Migration date: company announcements / Factiva
ModeratorPre-acquisition Brand Equity
The acquired brand's market strength before the deal		Euromonitor Passport
ControlsDeal value, deal timing, acquirer characteristics, target characteristics, and related transaction informationLSEG Workspace – Deals
Core relationshipMigration depth and speed → post-acquisition brand performance
Stronger acquired brands may be more vulnerable to rapid or extensive migration		Constructed by merging the sources above
Empirical Approach

Use the actual date of a visible brand change and compare market performance before and after the migration. Then compare different migration depths and speeds. If the same acquired brand is migrated at different times across countries, those timing differences can provide additional leverage for identifying the effect of brand migration.

\[ \text{Market Share} = \beta_1 \text{Migration Depth} + \beta_2 \text{Migration Speed} + \beta_3 \text{Target Brand Equity} + \text{Interactions} + \text{Controls} + \varepsilon \]
Contribution
  • Separates legal ownership transfer from consumer-held brand identity and shows that the two need not move at the same speed.
  • Translates the broad idea of post-acquisition integration into two observable marketing decisions: how much to change and how quickly to change it.
  • Directly addresses how much of the acquired brand's existing identity should be preserved and when deeper integration becomes worthwhile.
Target Journal
Journal of Marketing (JM)

4. The Brand Firewall

The Brand Firewall: How Brand Architecture Contains Corporate Reputation Spillovers
Target Journal: Journal of Marketing (JM)
Background
Brand architecture determines how reputation travels across a firm's portfolio. Stronger links between the corporate parent and product brands can make positive reputation and brand equity easier to share, but the same links may also amplify damage when the firm faces an environmental, labor, governance, or ethics controversy. Prior research has examined brand architecture and scandal spillovers separately. Less is known about whether pre-existing brand architecture systematically changes the impact of corporate controversies on real consumer demand.
Core Question
Can brand architecture shield product brands from negative corporate reputation spillovers?
Variables and Data
RoleVariable / DefinitionData Source
Dependent variableBrand market performance
Brand market share and changes around a corporate controversy		Euromonitor Passport
Main explanatory variableCorporate Controversy
Environmental, labor, governance, ethics, and other firm-level misconduct events		LSEG Workspace
ModeratorParent–Brand Linkage
How visibly the product brand is connected to the corporate parent		Brand names, websites, packaging, company materials + hand coding
Brand ownershipBrand–Parent Mapping
Links each consumer brand to its parent firm		LSEG Workspace + company materials
Core relationshipCorporate controversy → weaker brand performance
Stronger parent-brand linkage → larger expected decline		Constructed by merging the sources above
Empirical Approach

Use corporate controversies as focal events and compare brand performance before and after each event. More importantly, compare brands within the same company that differ in how visibly they are linked to the parent. This makes it easier to assess whether brand architecture amplifies or contains reputation spillovers.

\[ \text{Market Share} = \beta_1 \bigl( \text{Post-Controversy} \times \text{Parent--Brand Linkage} \bigr) + \text{Controls} + \varepsilon \]
Contribution
  • Recasts brand architecture from a static portfolio-design choice into a mechanism for allocating reputational risk.
  • Reveals a structural trade-off between sharing positive brand equity and containing negative reputation spillovers.
  • Provides direct implications for portfolio design, ESG risk, and crisis management in multi-brand firms.
Target Journal
Journal of Marketing (JM)
